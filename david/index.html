Reading the model

The given numbers don’t fix the shape. A 12′ back wall, a 4′ left wall and right angles at B and D leave one thing free. The front wall and the angles at A and C depend on the right wall length: front = √(right² + 128), with lengths in feet. Set the right wall to 4′ and the shed is a plain 12′×4′ rectangle. A longer right wall swings corner B out past the end of the back wall.

Heights are floor to the top of the wall framing, taken at the outside corner. That plane is where the rafters bear, so it rises at the roof pitch. The overhang is measured level, 12″ out from the outside face of the framing.

Which wall the rafters are square to decides which wall is level. Square to the back wall, the back top plate is level at 7′, and the front wall, which isn’t parallel to the back, rises along its length. Square to the front wall, the front plate is level and the back one slopes instead. The back wall’s low corner stays at 7′.

Either way, two walls get raked top plates. The plates of every wall that isn’t square to the rafters slope, and so do their stud tops. Toggle the rafter direction to see which walls those are, and read the stud lengths in the panel.
