Reading the model

The floor is the unusual part. Two 4× girders run the long direction on concrete block piers, with 4×4 joists laid across them and a ¾″ plywood deck on top. Four-by-four joists are heavy for a shed — most are framed with 2×4s or 2×6s on edge. Switch off the subfloor to see them.

The 2×4s at the joist ends are siding nailers, not structure. The wall plates bear on the deck, which the joists carry to the girders; the nailers hang off the joist ends and hold the bottom edge of the siding. They are also where the water damage is.

Plank roof sheathing, not plywood — individual boards with gaps between them, which is what dates the construction. Turn the shingles off to see it.

Roof spread is drawn as a mechanism, not as a shape. Switch on “Roof spread” and you get arrows for the suspected thrust and the rafter ties that would resist it — but the roof itself stays straight. The sag has not been measured, and a model bent by a guess would look authoritative about a number nobody has.

Count the siding joints. The panels are full 48″ sheets, so the joints are a 4-foot ruler. That is how the footprint gets measured without a tape — and why the model’s 8′×10′ is still marked an estimate.

Two layers are off by default because they do not exist yet: the 1× cellular PVC base band, and the pair of 48″ LED shop lights replacing the single hanging fluorescent.
