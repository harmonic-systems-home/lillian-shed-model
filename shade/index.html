How it works

The sun is computed, not looked up. Position comes from the NOAA solar equations for the 21st of the chosen month, which lands on or next to the solstices and equinoxes. It’s good to a few minutes and a fraction of a degree — far finer than a shade sail needs.

Time is clock time. The standard UTC offset and the daylight-saving rule turn it into sun time, so noon on the slider is not when the sun is highest. Solar noon is listed under the sun figures.

Everything is treated as solid. Real tree canopies and umbrella fabric let some light through, and sails block 80–95%. The ground is flat, and nothing outside the yard casts shade unless you add it — a neighbour’s house or a big tree can matter more than anything in the yard.

The day strip checks, every five minutes, whether a seated head over the patio table can see the sun. With several patio sets, it follows the one selected.

Sun position from the NOAA solar calculation; shadows drawn by the 3D renderer; hourly outlines projected along the sun’s rays. Nothing here leaves your browser.
